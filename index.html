THE REVIEW PROCESS

Three questions to ask

  1. 01
    Who published it?

    Look for a named author, a source, and the original date.

  2. 02
    What evidence is shown?

    Find the primary study, statement, document, or data behind the claim.

  3. 03
    Who else confirms it?

    Check independent reporting and corrections before sharing.

A polished writing style is not proof. An emotional style is not proof of falsehood.

    03 / UNDERSTAND

    What the score means

    FALCON v1 looks for transparent language cues: sensational phrasing, requests to share urgently, sweeping claims, vague authority, and emphatic formatting. Each detected cue adds a fixed number of points to a review priority score. The starting score is 10 and the maximum is 90.

    Topic detection uses a short keyword list. The dashboard groups only items you analyzed in this browser. A low score means fewer matched cues; it does not mean a claim is true. The research write-up also proposes BERT classification, external fact checks, live streams, and federated learning. Those components require the missing original code, trained model, data, and credentials and are not simulated as completed features.
